How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of staggered pegs: each row is one peg wider than the row above. A ball landing at the top has an even chance of moving left or right, and it repeats that split at every peg row it touches. After the final row, it lands in one of the pockets along the bottom — one more pocket than the number of rows.

Because each path is a chain of fifty-fifty deflections, balls gather near the middle and only rarely drift all the way to an edge. Statisticians know this as a Galton board, and the shape it makes is a binomial distribution. The payout table simply flips that curve: common centre pockets pay less, while rarer outer pockets pay more. Six words worth knowing

Pocket
A slot at the bottom of the board that holds the multiplier a finished ball collects.
Rows
The number of peg lines a ball crosses; more rows mean more pockets and steeper odds.
Risk level
A preset that stretches or flattens the payout table without changing the long-run return much.
House margin
The small gap between true odds and paid odds that funds the operator.
Provably fair
A system of published hashes that lets players check a result was not altered.
Variance
The scale of the swings around the average result — the part the risk switch really changes.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On licensed platforms, yes. Each deflection comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you confirm afterwards that the path was fixed before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes rise while their chance gets smaller. The overall return stays broadly the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk smooths the ride, high risk concentrates value in rare edge hits. Choose according to how much swing your budget and temperament can handle.

Can I practise without spending anything?

Yes. The board on this page runs on virtual credits, and most operators keep a free mode with the same odds. The maths does not change when real money is added.

Does timing or button rhythm influence where a ball lands?

No. The path is set by the random generator, not by when or how quickly you press drop. Any pattern you notice is coincidence, not a system.
